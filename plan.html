Status: Planning Only Updated 2026-10-13 Gymnasium • SB3 • Alpaca

Modular Swing-Trading RL Project Plan

A provider-independent, causal research pipeline for US equities swing trading using Stable-Baselines3 (PPO, SAC, TD3), customizable Gymnasium environments with learned stop/target zones, chronological walk-forward validation, and Alpaca paper execution.

System Pipeline Flow: End-to-End Architecture

01 Intended Outcome and Starting Assumptions

Baseline Foundation

Build a provider-independent research pipeline that accumulates market and company data, generates causal features, produces chronological training/validation/test datasets, trains interchangeable PPO, SAC, and TD3 agents, and exports frozen models for inference against live market data and Alpaca paper trading.

The final model proposes position size, long/short direction, stop loss, a learned take-profit zone, and position adjustments. Execution and risk controls validate those proposals before sending orders. Add features and reward terms gradually, measuring each addition against the previous version.

Initial Planning Defaults (Configurable before implementation)

1. Swing Trading Cadence Daily + Weekly

US equities and swing trading; begin with daily decisions and completed daily bars, then add completed weekly context. Track holding periods in sessions. A research target of roughly 2–20 sessions is an observation goal, not a rule that blocks protective exits.

2. Execution & Fill Timing Next-Session Open

Generate a signal after the completed session is available; simulate entry/adjustment at the next regular-session open with costs. Paper inference uses the same timing and accounts for feed publication delays. Protective orders can operate between daily decisions.

3. Universe & Sharing Single-Symbol Episodes

Start with a small declared universe and one traded symbol per episode. Share the policy across symbols with separate episodes; add simultaneous portfolio allocation as a separate environment version with explicit allocation constraints.

4. Incremental Complexity Price → Stops → Short

Begin with price/volume and a simple reward. Add learned stops/targets before the paper-execution milestone; introduce shorting after long-only accounting works.

5. Staged Deployment Offline → Shadow → Paper

Offline research, then live-data shadow inference, then Alpaca paper orders. Real-money deployment is a separate future task. This change creates documentation only.

03 Modular Data Accumulator and Dataset Generator

Data Infrastructure

Contracts and Storage

Define separate record types for bars, trades, quotes, company classifications, filings/facts, options chains, news, and corporate actions. Provider capabilities describe supported record types, date ranges, granularity, feeds, delays, free-tier quotas, licensing, and point-in-time guarantees. Unsupported requests fail clearly rather than returning a silently substituted feed.

Every record includes symbol/instrument ID, provider, source event time, public availability time, retrieval time, revision/as-of identity, and applicable currency/units. For initial live snapshots, availability is no earlier than actual retrieval. Historical adapters may reconstruct earlier availability only from documented publication/filing metadata; uncertain timestamps require a conservative lag or exclusion. Record uncertainty in the manifest.

Accumulate immutable raw responses plus checksums. Normalize into partitioned Parquet, cache locally, and query with DuckDB or an equivalent light engine. Incremental jobs use resumable cursors, pagination, deduplication, bounded retries, and provider-specific quota budgets. Revised data creates a new version rather than mutating an experiment's inputs.

The generator builds aligned feature matrices, validity/age masks, chronological splits, fitted preprocessing, and a dataset manifest. Synthetic data generators produce deterministic accounting/leakage fixtures and stress scenarios; synthetic profits are not evidence of a market edge. Training episodes may be sampled within the training split without mixing chronological split membership.

04 Preventing Lookahead Bias

Causal Rigor
Causal Timeline: Bar Completion, As-Of Join, and Fill Ordering
  • Backward As-Of Joins by Availability Time: Use availability-time backward as-of joins: a feature used at decision time must have been observable by that time. Store bar start/end separately, including publication delay.
  • Session-Completed Boundaries: Daily indicators use completed sessions. Weekly/monthly features become available only after those periods close; Monday's signal cannot use Friday's future close. Intraday resampling respects exchange sessions, holidays, and DST. No backfilling incomplete higher-timeframe indicators.
  • Trailing Windows Only: EMA, RSI, ATR, returns, and rolling volume use trailing windows. Swing pivots that need future bars become available only after confirmation; avoid centered rolling windows and future peak/trough labels as observations.
  • Point-in-Time Financial Statements: Financial features use original filing availability and the latest eligible vintage at that decision. Later amendments/restatements and today's Company Facts summary must not overwrite what the agent knew then. Resolve period duration and duplicate concepts/units explicitly.
  • Options OI & Sentiment Timestamps: Options OI uses its own published as-of date and availability, not quote time. News uses first-seen/publication times with lag and revision handling. Archive sector classifications and universe membership; current winners/constituents are not a historical universe.
  • Global Calendar Splits (60 / 20 / 20): Split globally by calendar time across all symbols, initially approximately 60/20/20 subject to enough history. Training-only preprocessing; freeze categorical vocabulary and handle unknown sectors. Validation/test may use earlier historical bars only as causal indicator warmup, never future outcomes or fitted data.
  • Purge Gap at Split Boundaries: Define a conservative purge at split ends for positions/labels whose outcome would cross into the next split; use a declared research maximum holding horizon for this purpose. Keep portfolio states and replay buffers separate. Audit every split's observable times and episode bounds.
  • Strict Validation Discipline & Locked Test: Repeated checkpoint selection uses validation and designated walk-forward folds. Unlock the final test only after features, reward, and selection rules are frozen. If test results influence redesign, retire that test as a development interval and reserve a new holdout.

Incremental Ablation

Each feature has name/version, inputs, parameters, units, timeframe, warmup, availability lag, missing-value policy, and incremental/batch parity checks. One family is introduced per experiment; snapshot feature schema changes require retraining and a new model bundle.

Call/Put Wall Proxy Definition: Define a call/put wall initially as a reproducible OI concentration proxy: aggregate separately by type and a configured expiry bucket, find selected high-OI strikes, and express their distance from spot. Save aggregation/tie rules and stale-data limits. 06 Environment and Learned Trade Outputs

Gymnasium Core

Action and Decision Contracts

Use a continuous action design supported by all three algorithms. First implement a position-only baseline; the final action schema is a normalized four-component Box:

Action Component Decoded Meaning and Geometry
Signed target exposure Negative = short, zero = flat, positive = long; magnitude specifies requested equity allocation.
Stop distance Positive bounded volatility-relative distance; produces a stop below a long or above a short reference price.
Take-profit near distance Learned positive distance to the near boundary of the profit zone, above a long or below a short reference price.
Take-profit zone width Learned positive width beyond the near boundary; defines the far boundary.
Learned Trade Geometry: Action Space to Order Levels

No Hardcoded Reward-to-Risk Ratios: The network determines the target zone; no hardcoded reward-to-risk ratio substitutes for that prediction. Use causal ATR or a declared volatility scale to decode distances, with configurable bounds, minimum ticks, and rounding. Save both raw policy outputs and validated outputs. Warmup/invalid ATR prevents a trade. Actual ATR bounds and exposure limits are experiment settings, not proven optimal values.

Typed Decision Contract: Export a typed decision with symbol, decision/expiry timestamps, model/schema IDs, reference price, requested/approved exposure, share/notional amount, long/short/flat direction, stop price, target-zone lower/upper prices, and resulting add/reduce/close/reverse/hold intent. Size is calculated from known equity and prices, then capped by risk/buying-power/liquidity constraints. Repeated unchanged target exposure must not add another position.

Protection Anchoring and Reversals: Keep existing protections anchored to the existing entry unless a model decision explicitly requests a valid update. Define a deadband/minimum order size to avoid tiny adjustments. Record any rejected or clipped proposal. Direction reversals close and reconcile the existing position before opening the other direction; protective exits override holding-period preferences.

Making a Zone Affect Outcomes

Start learned exits with stop plus one learned target; then promote to the four-component zone schema. For zone experiments, use a fixed, configurable scale-out schedule such as half at the near boundary and half at the far boundary, with protection maintained on remaining shares. Both boundaries must influence outcomes so the network has a reason to learn zone width. Whole-share rounding and small-position fallback are explicit. Any future partial-exit allocations are a separate action-schema change.

Simulate fills, gaps, spread/slippage, commissions/fees, borrow costs, short availability, overnight exposure, dividends, and corporate actions. The next open is a future fill outcome, never an input to yesterday's decision. Stops/targets are decoded from the recorded decision reference; if a gap invalidates their geometry before entry, follow a declared reject/skip policy shared by simulation and paper execution.

Use finer bars/trades for execution replay when available while retaining daily decisions. If one OHLC bar hits a stop and target with unknown order, use a declared conservative stop-first rule and report sensitivity to that assumption. Do not claim stop prices guarantee fill prices during gaps. Episode termination/truncation and final valuation must be documented and checker-compatible.

07 Reward Design

P&L Accounting

Begin with per-step log change in equity after modeled execution/holding costs. Positive and negative P&L are already rewarded/punished; do not double-count a second realized-trade profit reward. Invalid/ruined equity requires explicit terminal handling rather than an undefined log. Account equity is the source of truth, with a decomposed reward log.

Stage Candidate Addition Reason and Control Mechanism
R0 Net equity return only Establish accounting and a transparent baseline.
R1 Modest turnover penalty Discourage excess adjustment if real costs alone do not. Penalize traded notional, not every inference call; record how much exceeds actual costs.
R2 Drawdown/downside-exposure term Test one at a time for large adverse positions. Avoid summing realized/unrealized losses twice or repeatedly penalizing the same unchanged drawdown.
R3 Holding-frequency preference Consider only after holding-duration and turnover reports show a need. Holding age belongs in state; swing behavior can emerge from daily cadence, costs, and trading constraints. No reward for merely staying in a losing trade.
Diagnostic Win/loss ratio, profit factor, expectancy Report initially, not optimize directly. High win rate can hide large losses; trade-count ratios are sparse, unstable with few trades, and easy to game. Any later reward experiment must show improvement in net risk-adjusted results.
🚨

Hard Limits Outside Shaping: Fix hard position/order/exposure limits outside reward shaping; a learned penalty is not a substitute for enforcement. Freeze reward coefficients before the final test and record every change as a new experiment.

09 Checkpoint Evaluation and Visual Reports

Validation Protocol

At each configured checkpoint (initially every 10k environment transitions), evaluate a separate validation environment with frozen preprocessing and deterministic inference. Account for vectorization when scheduling callbacks. Save latest, periodic, and best-by-validation checkpoints; do not repeatedly run the final test during training. A custom selector should use declared portfolio metrics, not automatically treat SB3's highest shaped reward as the best strategy. (SB3 callbacks).

Checkpoint Evaluation, Metric Selection & Model Freezing Lifecycle

Mandatory Visual Plotly HTML Reports (Backed by Machine-Readable Tables)

1. Validation vs Steps Selection

Validation performance versus training steps, with all checkpoints/seeds and the selected checkpoint clearly marked.

2. Equity & Drawdowns P&L Curves

Equity, benchmark equity, underwater/drawdown curves, net returns, and annual risk-adjusted metrics.

3. Price & Zone Overlays Trade Geometry

Price charts with long/short entries, adjustments, exits, stop levels, and learned target-zone bands; track changes through time.

4. Holding & Distributions Diagnostics

Holding-time and trade-return distributions, win rate plus average win/loss and expectancy, turnover/cost breakdown, exposure and rejected decisions.

5. Comparative Matrix Seed Variance

Algorithm/config/feature/reward comparisons on matched windows, with variability across seeds and market regimes.

6. Paper Parity Reports Execution Drift

Later paper reports comparing expected and actual fills, execution latency, and feature feed drift.

📋

Unbiased Test Audit: Reports must label training, validation, held-out test, and paper results distinctly, and show weak/failed runs as well as the winner. The final test is an unbiased audit of an already selected frozen candidate; it does not select a new best model.

10 Saving and Freezing the Model

Bundle Integrity

Freeze a deployable inference bundle containing model weights, fitted preprocessing/normalization state, exact feature/action/reward versions, symbol universe and provider/feed assumptions, resolved configs, dependency lock, data/split manifests, validation-selection rule/results, and checksums. Attach final test results as a versioned report without retuning the selected weights. Mark failures as candidates that are not eligible for paper execution.

Inference never calls learn(), updates normalization, or appends to a training replay buffer. Use deterministic predictions for the first paper protocol. Verify save/load decision parity on fixed observations, reject schema/provider mismatches, and record the immutable bundle ID with each signal. Store resumable training checkpoints separately from deployment bundles. (SB3 normalization).

11 Live-Data Evaluation and Alpaca Paper Execution

Paper Trading

Use a persistent local/server inference process; Colab is the training backend and is not assumed to provide unattended execution uptime. Consume live market data through the same feature code, honoring delays and completed-bar boundaries. Shadow mode records decisions without orders and checks historical/incremental feature parity, scheduling, latency, and missing/stale data behavior.

Then use Alpaca's explicit paper mode and verify the endpoint/account. Query tradability, shortability/borrow eligibility, buying power, tick/quantity restrictions, and supported order combinations. Shorting is conditional on actual account and asset eligibility. Never assume arbitrary fractional short orders or short-borrow history is available. (Alpaca Margin & Short Selling).

Alpaca Execution Architecture: Two-Boundary Zone Lifecycle vs Standard Bracket

Broker-Held Protection & Scale-Out Order Lifecycle: Use broker-held protection where supported. Alpaca brackets have a single take-profit leg and do not support extended hours; a two-boundary scale-out zone needs a separately verified lifecycle design. Do not pretend a normal bracket implements two profit targets. Test stop coverage, partial fills, leg quantity updates, replacement/cancellation races, and rejection recovery before enabling zone execution. On adjustments, reconcile positions and outstanding exits before changing quantities. (Alpaca Order Capabilities).

State Persistence & Idempotency: Persist decision/order IDs, desired and actual positions, executions, and bundle IDs. Use idempotent decision processing, reconnect/backfill handling, bounded retries, and reconcile uncertain order submissions before resubmission. On restart reconstruct state from broker positions/open orders plus durable records. Stop new exposure on stale data, schema mismatch, failed reconciliation, or configured risk-limit breaches while maintaining existing protection.

Evaluation Criteria: Predeclare position/exposure/turnover/loss limits and paper evaluation criteria before a run. Report net returns, drawdowns, holding duration, trade count, costs, operational errors, and drift over enough time/trades to evaluate the protocol; there is no universal duration that proves profitability. Paper fills omit some real market effects. (Alpaca Paper Limitations).

12 Colab, Efficiency, and Reproducibility

Compute & Runtime

Use the official Google Colab CLI with pinned version and checked subcommand help. Package/upload or install the project and its config/data explicitly. Record runtime hardware and dependencies; begin with CPU and profile before requesting GPU. Persist periodic checkpoints and export artifacts/logs before releasing only the sessions created for the job. (Official Google Colab CLI • Colab Runtime Limits & FAQ).

Precompute causal features once per dataset version, use arrays in the environment loop, query partitioned storage for research, and batch/cache provider calls within quotas. Benchmark single-environment versus vectorized runs rather than assuming subprocesses help. Bound replay memory from actual observation/action dimensions; tune collection/update ratios with environment count. Keep secrets out of scripts, CLI arguments, outputs, and bundles.
